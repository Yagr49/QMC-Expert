2.1 Training guidance NN models

Expert guidance datasets were constructed through systematic literature exploration followed by independent blind classification by three domain experts. Each reported compound was assigned an activity label ("Bad" or "Good") based on expert assessment of its antibacterial profile. A biocide agent was labeled "Good" based on objective correlation with its broad spectrum of antimicrobial activity. Additional criteria (e.g., cytotoxicity, cost, sustainability) were considered where possible. Disputed cases were resolved by majority among three experts. This procedure yielded a curated collection of 1,500 labeled compounds encompassing diverse antibacterial activity data.

To identify the optimal descriptor for downstream expert-guided learning, we benchmarked four molecular representations under a unified 5-fold stratified cross-validation protocol: transformer-based embeddings (MolFormer, 768-dimensional; ChemBERTa) and classical fingerprints (Morgan, MACCS). MolFormer embeddings consistently outperformed all alternatives, achieving the highest mean F1-score of 0.655 (MLP with StandardScaler) and accuracy of 0.856, whereas Morgan, MACCS, and ChemBERTa exhibited near-random F1 scores (≤0.50) despite moderate accuracy (Figure SI 1-2). This accuracy–F1 divergence indicates that classical fingerprints and the smaller chemical language model capture majority-class bias rather than biocide-specific structural motifs, whereas MolFormer’s pre-trained chemical vocabulary already encodes the subtle physicochemical nuances relevant to membrane-targeting cationics. Detailed per-representation and per-scaler comparisons are provided in the Supporting Information. 

Building on the MolFormer baseline, we trained a dedicated MLP-based encoder (512 → 384 → 256 neurons) with a supervised contrastive objective to distill the implicit “chemical intuition” embedded in the expert labels into a compact, task-specific latent space. To address the inherent class imbalance of the expert-annotated dataset, we employed Borderline SMOTE (sampling strategy = 0.8) during encoder training. The model was optimized with a hard-negative contrastive loss, which explicitly pushes hard misclassified negatives away from the anchor embedding, thereby sharpening inter-class boundaries. The resulting 256-dimensional representations are referred to as human embeddings hereafter.

Figure 2 presents t-SNE projections (perplexity = 30) of the held-out test fold before and after contrastive refinement. Prior to expert-guided training, active and inactive compounds were heavily intermingled (silhouette score = 0.007). After contrastive learning, the two classes resolved into distinct spatial regions, with the silhouette score rising to 0.108—a 15-fold improvement in cluster separability. This visual trend was corroborated by complementary internal validation metrics: the Davies–Bouldin index decreased from 6.54 to 2.58, while the Calinski–Harabasz score increased from 3.60 to 22.08 (Figure SI 4). Collectively, these metrics confirm that the encoder reorganized the latent space so that molecules with similar antibacterial behavior map to proximal regions.
