We submit our Article, “Human versus Quantitative Supervision for Antibacterial Molecular Representation Learning,” for consideration in the Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling.

The manuscript addresses when coarse human Good/Bad annotations provide transferable supervision for antibacterial molecular representations, and how their value compares with numerical biological measurements. Using quaternary ammonium compounds and the Q-HERO chemical collection, we distinguish direct potency ranking, practical competition after downstream refitting, and the effect of supervision replacement within a fixed learning procedure.

Partial MolFormer fine-tuning produces Q-HERO downstream R² of 0.269 with expert labels compared with 0.312 and 0.308 for molecule-level and record-level MIC supervision. Complementary matched-molecule interventions include continuous and binary MIC, shuffled-label controls, raw pretrained features and untrained encoders. Tests across four frozen backbones and a composition-fixed numerical-context comparison expose the conditions and limits of the human-label effect. We report the adverse and unresolved results alongside the positive comparisons and confine causal interpretation to the computational interventions.

The contribution is an experimentally grounded comparison of supervision sources and evaluation regimes for chemical representation learning. We believe this methodological question is relevant to JCIM readers working on molecular machine learning, activity prediction and the incorporation of expert assessment into modelling.
