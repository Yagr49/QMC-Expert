• # Editorial and Scientific Review (read-only)

  ## (1) Concrete corrections

  1. **"Practically competitive" is an evaluative claim without a criterion.** §3.1: "The expert and quantitative sources therefore produce practically competitive representations in this refitting protocol"; echoed in the Abstract and Conclusions ("produces practically competitive downstream Q-HERO representations"). No practical margin or equivalence test is defined, sources differ in size and update budget, and the intervals are exploratory with three seeds. *Correction:* report the contrast descriptively — expert supervision attains Q-HERO R² = 0.269 versus 0.312/0.308 under identical refitting — and either define the criterion for "competitive" or delete the term. Similarly, Conclusions' "direct expert scores track measured potency" should be "show positive rank agreement (Spearman 0.397) with measured potency."

  2. **§3.1: "Human targets thus encode learnable potency-related information, while numerical supervision provides stronger direct ordering."** The 0.397 vs 0.547/0.612 comparison establishes weaker ordering, not learnability, and no shuffled/permutation null is reported for direct transfer. *Correction:* anchor the learnability claim in the matched human-vs-shuffled contrast of §3.2 — e.g., "direct expert scores show positive rank agreement (0.397), consistent with the label-assignment effect demonstrated under matched frozen-feature training."

  3. **"Transfer" is used ambiguously.** Abstract: "pooled MIC also transfers better than exact *S. aureus* MIC"; §3.4 heading: "Quantitative context changes transfer on identical source chemistry." "Transfer" collides with the paper's term of art "direct transfer" (source-trained head, no refitting); the §3.4 contrast is frozen-feature supervision followed by downstream scaffold-CV refitting. *Correction:* "under matched frozen-feature training and downstream scaffold-CV refitting, pooled-MIC supervision yields lower Q-HERO RMSE (1.525) than exact *S. aureus* MIC (1.617)."

  4. **Undefined comparator.** §3.1: "The six-descriptor reference reaches Q-HERO R² = 0.057" — no such baseline is defined in Methods. *Correction:* define it in §2.3 (descriptor set, fitting protocol) or remove it; results should not depend on an undefined comparator.

  5. **Uncertainty for single-run arms is unexplained.** §2.6: bootstrap replicates "resample source initialisation seeds and whole target cation-scaffold groups," but Table 2 gives raw features as "one fixed decoder run" (untrained likewise). Seed resampling is degenerate for single-run arms, yet §3.2 reports expert-vs-raw intervals (+0.005 [−0.102, +0.115]). *Correction:* state explicitly that single-run-arm uncertainty comes from scaffold-group resampling only, or these paired intervals are ambiguous.

  6. **Causal terminology needs tightening.** The bounded estimand in §2.6 ("change in predictive loss under a specified replacement of supervision within a fixed computational procedure") is appropriate, and the disclaimers (no mechanism, expert-status effect, or bias removal) are correct and should be retained verbatim. But the Abstract's "locate the causal interpretation in controlled changes to the training procedure" is vague, and real-vs-shuffled is more precisely a controlled label-assignment (abolition) intervention than a general "label intervention" (§1). *Correction:* "we restrict causal claims to controlled replacements of supervision within a fixed training procedure."

  7. **Front-matter coherence.** Affiliation markers are fused to surnames ("Ilina," "Frolova," "Seferyana," "Sonc"), mismatching the Contributions ("Ilin," "Frolov," "Seferyan," "Son"); Medvedev carries "*" but no corresponding email is listed. Fix superscript rendering and complete corresponding-author details.

  ## (2) Remaining limits

  - Annotation provenance (H1–H7) is pending: with only final labels, inter-rater agreement is uncomputable, and whether annotators saw the MIC later used for quantitative supervision (H4) is unresolved — this bounds any reading of the labels as independent chemical intuition, as the draft itself notes (§1, §3.1).
  - Three (partial fine-tuning) and five (frozen) source seeds; intervals conditional on fixed target folds; exploratory and multiplicity-unadjusted.
  - The human-vs-shuffled contrast is an abolition control: it shows the procedure exploits the real mapping, but with the expert-minus-continuous interval spanning zero, human-specific information beyond MIC is unestablished. §3.2's separation of these questions is correct and should be preserved.
  - Q-HERO lacks row-level strain/laboratory provenance; the replicate audit (38 strata) is selective and cannot support broad reproducibility claims in either direction.
  - OOD-17 is 17 compounds with negative R²; external validity is minimal.
  - The chemistry appendix lacks original spectra (reserved for coauthors); JCIM requires complete characterization data for synthesized compounds at submission.

  ## (3) Positioning for JCIM

  The genuine contribution is methodological — a clean separation of transferable signal, practical competition, and incremental value over pretrained features, with matched interventions and archived reproducibility — well suited to JCIM's methods audience. The explicit disclaimers (no equivalence, no preregistration, no denoising, no independent architectural replication) are strengths. Recommendations: (i) foreground the three-question design in the abstract rather than QAC-specific numbers; (ii) complete H1–H7 and D1–D2 before submission, since annotation protocol and data availability are review blockers; (iii) frame the QAC testbed as a case study for the evaluation framework, not a domain-general result; (iv) the §3.5 suggestion of a matched-molecule partial-fine-tuning experiment and richer annotation/assay metadata is appropriate future work — keep it clearly marked as motivation, not evidence.
