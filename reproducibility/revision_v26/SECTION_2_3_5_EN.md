The quantitative OOD comparison uses all 17 compounds and the four MIC/MBC endpoints against S. aureus and E. coli. Each target predictor is fitted on the original 105-QAC cohort before the new compounds are evaluated. Of the 68 endpoint records, 67 are exact and IT505 E. coli MBC is reported as >500 mg/L. We retain this bound separately and exclude it from exact-error scores, rather than treating 500 mg/L as a measured concentration. Errors are evaluated on the log₂(mg/L) scale. The strict source-matched follow-ups exclude target cations from encoder-training panels; their frozen-source prediction protocol is distinct from the historical few-shot analysis retained in Figure S33.

For the archived v5 compact human encoder, the new fixed-feature decoder calculation gives pooled OOD RMSE 2.023 with text task codes, versus 2.245 for raw MolFormer under the same text-code decoder protocol. These are point estimates from one archived source checkpoint; no source-run SD is available. Historical source overlap with the original QAC targets distinguishes this reference from the source-excluded controls below. Full task-coding and endpoint results are in Tables S35–S36 and S49. The full-corpus quantitative checkpoint from the original Table S46 is not substituted by the available QAC-only ablation.

The source-matched compact encoders from Section2.2 provide a stricter test of supervisory content. With fixed categorical task codes, OOD RMSE is 2.268 ± 0.077 for human guidance, 2.276 ± 0.095 for binary MIC guidance and 2.474 ± 0.131 for continuous MIC guidance; the fixed raw reference gives 2.504. Here ± is sample SD across five source runs. Shuffled-human supervision gives 2.172 ± 0.112, so these results do not establish an advantage of meaningful human-label assignment. Human minus raw has ΔRMSE −0.236, with conditional four-family bootstrap interval [−0.524, −0.016], whereas human minus shuffled has +0.096 [−0.143, +0.283]. Thus the representation-learning procedure can improve the raw reference without demonstrating a uniquely human semantic effect.

A separate backbone experiment tests whether grouped-validation performance persists under the new chemical families. Full fine-tuning gives OOD RMSE 3.032 ± 0.408, LoRA 2.756 ± 0.172 and the frozen pretrained backbone with a linear head 2.560 ± 0.206. Human-guided partial-backbone direct prediction gives 2.412 ± 0.088, compared with 2.106 ± 0.071 for frozen human-guided source features decoded with CatBoost. These five routes use three runs, and the two human routes share source checkpoints. The direct-minus-decoder point contrast is +0.306, with conditional four-family interval [−0.278, +0.971]. The lower decoder point loss is therefore not a resolved generalisation advantage at this sample size, and the route comparison changes decoding and adaptation together.

Figure 9 shows the observed OOD error distributions for the compact and backbone experiments in separate panels. The 17 molecules cover four scaffold families rather than 67 independent biological replicates. Endpoint errors within one compound and across runs are dependent, and variation over four families remains poorly determined. Censor sensitivity, per-endpoint scores and complete run statistics are reported in SI. These results support conditional predictive usefulness under the stated protocols; they do not establish universal superiority or equivalence of human and quantitative supervision.
