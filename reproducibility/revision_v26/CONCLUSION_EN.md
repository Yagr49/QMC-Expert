We combined expert guidance with pretrained molecular features to predict four antibacterial MIC/MBC endpoints for QACs and evaluated transfer to 17 newly synthesised compounds from four chemical families. The reference results show that expert-guided compact embeddings can support competitive quantitative prediction when combined with a task-aware decoder.

The completed source-matched experiments refine this conclusion. Human and quantitative guidance can yield useful representations, but their ordering depends on architecture, decoder and evaluation protocol. Shuffled labels remain competitive for the compact encoder, whereas particular partial-backbone experiments, including Q-HERO scaffold classification, support a contribution from meaningful label assignment. Statistical equivalence, universal superiority and a label-to-measurement exchange rate are not established.

Full fine-tuning improves some grouped target scores but gives higher OOD error under the tested budget; frozen human-guided features with CatBoost give the lower OOD point loss among the tested backbone routes. The small four-family sample limits uncertainty assessment. Geometry, SHAP and computational controls clarify feature reuse and prediction dependence without identifying an antibacterial mechanism. Structured expert labels are therefore a practical supervision source whose value should be assessed with matched numerical and shuffled controls, alongside complete annotation provenance.
