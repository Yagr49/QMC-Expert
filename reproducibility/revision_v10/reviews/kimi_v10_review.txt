• ## Review basis

  I reviewed only the supplied protocol text, results tables, and audit summaries, as an exploratory extension of v9. I accept the standing corrections: the protocol was exploratory, not preregistered; archived three-seed direct means and the separate seed-0 bootstrap are distinct estimators and must not be merged in any uncertainty statement.

  ## Scientifically defensible framing

  1. **Claim only within-backbone supervision contrasts.** The design supports statements of the form "on backbone X, arm A outperformed arm B on scaffold-held-out Q-HERO-405." Cross-backbone comparisons confound architecture, input dimension, and pretraining corpus; the protocol already concedes this, and the text must hold that line.
  2. **The strongest human-signal statement is convergent direction, not significance.** Two independent backbones (MoLFormer and c3-MoLFormer) agree that continuous quantitative MIC supervision > expert ≈ binary ≈ untrained, each beating its own shuffle. That triangulation is defensible as an exploratory, descriptive finding. It is not confirmatory: no multiplicity adjustment, and the key expert-vs-continuous intervals barely contain or exclude zero.
  3. **ChemBERTa-100M is the control that prevents overclaiming.** A null backbone across every arm shows "modern molecular models" do not universally help; the effect is backbone- and pretraining-dependent. Any sentence implying general superiority of modern architectures is contradicted by your own table.
  4. **Heterogeneity: your own audit argues against you.** The QMR replicate audit (38 same-compound/same-strain strata; median range 0; 94.7% within one twofold dilution; 279 within-group strain strata median range 1) indicates the Wuest–Minbiole programme is internally consistent. The defensible framing is therefore *not* "inter-lab heterogeneity drives differences" but "endpoint construction and aggregation/organism policy materially affect transfer," with the context-arm contrast explicitly confounded (organism mixture, censoring, N, aggregation all vary). Without row-level strain/protocol/lab identifiers, no heterogeneity rate — pooled conflict or otherwise — is estimable.
  5. **Causality must be scoped to the intervention performed.** Shuffled controls support label-correspondence specificity: the improvement depends on the true label pairing, a causal statement about the *supervision intervention*. Nothing here manipulates molecules, strains, or biology; biological causal mechanism claims are out of scope, as the protocol states.
  6. **External validity caveats are load-bearing.** Unknown pretraining membership of evaluation compounds (possible pretraining leakage), frozen probes rather than fine-tuned leaderboards, and single-seed raw-backbone baselines must accompany any external statement about Q-HERO.

  ## Contradictions and items requiring author response

  1. **molformer `quant-shuffled` is anomalous (+0.124), essentially equal to real expert (0.122), while `expert-shuffled` is −0.080 and c3's `quant-shuffled` is −0.062.** A shuffled-label control performing at real-arm level demands an explanation (chance feature–label structure with discrete MIC labels? near-neighbor leakage across scaffold splits? a different shuffle convention for continuous arms?). Until resolved, both halves of your specificity argument are weakened, and the continuous-over-expert advantage could partly reflect target marginal/shape artifacts rather than information. This is the single most important issue.
  2. **Duplicated rows: "context" and "matched-sa" panels contain identical point estimates** (expert, quant-continuous/quant-sa, untrained). If these are the same runs under two labels, say so; as printed, the table implies independent replication that does not exist. "matched-sa" also lacks a binary arm and borrows context shuffles, making the "matched" label misleading.
  3. **No bootstrap for the practically decisive contrast: `quant-continuous` minus `raw` backbone.** Raw MoLFormer (0.128) matches expert (0.122); c3 raw (0.076) exceeds its expert (0.010). So "expert supervision transfers" is not supported relative to unsupervised features, and "quantitative supervision adds signal beyond raw features" — your best claim — has no uncertainty interval. Add it before drawing conclusions.
  4. **Borderline intervals presented without adjustment.** `quant-pooled` minus `quant-sa` (upper CI −0.001) and expert minus quant-continuous (lower CI −0.0007) across ~30 exploratory contrasts must be reported as directional, not as evidence.
  5. **Estimator hygiene with v9:** label seed+scaffold conditional bootstrap intervals distinctly from the archived seed-0 bootstrap; do not pool or graph them as one estimator family.
  6. **Naming and capacity:** keep explicit that c3's "1.1B" counts pretraining molecules, not parameters; restate that input-dimension differences make this not a parameter-count experiment.
  7. **ChemFM-1B is pending;** no cross-backbone synthesis, averaging, or "modern models" summary is valid yet. Also, the ChemFM causal-encoder pooling convention is asserted but unverifiable from these materials; keep it in methods, not claims.
  8. **Minor:** raw-backbone arms are single-seed with no uncertainty; the untrained-encoder arms nearly match expert on both informative backbones, which the discussion should acknowledge (encoder pretraining contributes little here relative to label quality).

  ## Suggested results paragraph

  Across the two informative backbones, supervision contrasts were directionally consistent on scaffold-held-out Q-HERO-405 (n=405; 29 scaffolds; five seeds; ΔRMSE = RMSE(first arm) − RMSE(second arm), negative favors the first arm). With MoLFormer features, continuous quantitative MIC supervision achieved R²=0.237 (RMSE 1.50), versus 0.122 (1.61) for expert labels, 0.079 for prevalence-matched binary MIC, 0.055 for the untrained encoder, and 0.128 for raw frozen features; expert labels outperformed their shuffle (ΔRMSE −0.175, 95% CI −0.322 to −0.024), continuous MIC outperformed its shuffle (−0.107, CI −0.228 to −0.016), and continuous MIC was directionally better than expert (+0.109, CI −0.001 to 0.226). c3-MoLFormer reproduced this ordering (continuous 0.145; expert 0.010; binary 0.004; untrained 0.009; raw 0.076; continuous vs shuffle −0.182, CI −0.299 to −0.029; expert vs shuffle −0.113, CI −0.246 to −0.012; continuous vs expert +0.120, CI −0.010 to 0.228). ChemBERTa-100M was null throughout (all R² between −0.16 and −0.11; no interval excluded 0), demonstrating backbone dependence rather than universal benefit. In the 529-molecule context panel, expert labels underperformed raw frozen features (ΔRMSE +0.185, CI 0.050 to 0.404); pooled quantitative MIC (R²=0.209) exceeded exact single-organism S. aureus MIC (0.110) by ΔRMSE −0.092 (CI −0.159 to −0.001), a contrast confounded by organism mixture, censoring, sample size, and aggregation policy. All comparisons are exploratory, without multiplicity adjustment.

  ## Suggested conclusion paragraph

  In this exploratory extension, the defensible conclusion is narrow: on two independent modern backbones, continuous quantitative MIC supervision transferred to scaffold-held-out Q-HERO-405 better than binary or expert supervision, with label-correspondence specificity shown against shuffled controls; raw unsupervised MoLFormer features matched expert supervision, ChemBERTa-100M showed no signal, and the internal replicate audit indicates the source programme is consistent, implicating endpoint construction and aggregation policy — not demonstrable lab heterogeneity — in cross-dataset differences. These are associations from frozen-feature probes with unknown pretraining overlap; they do not establish biological causality, human denoising, equivalence, or universal competitiveness of modern architectures. The positive `quant-shuffled` anomaly must be resolved, and the quantitative-minus-raw contrast and pending ChemFM-1B results reported, before any external claim about Q-HERO is warranted.
